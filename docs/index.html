Content refresh prioritization from search & content signals.

Abstract

Five-sentence summary

This study asks whether observed content and search-performance signals can help prioritize pages for human review for a possible content refresh.

I compared a simple three-signal rule-based baseline with a Logistic Regression classifier using page, search, and recent performance features.

The evaluation used an 80/20 split grouped by client, with Average Precision and ROC-AUC measured on the same held-out test set.

Logistic Regression measured higher Average Precision (0.5582 vs 0.4974) and ROC-AUC (0.5621 vs 0.4644) than the Week-4 baseline.

These results are directional decision-support evidence under the evaluated conditions, not proof that a refresh will improve future search performance.

01 · Question

What decision should the model support?

Research question: Can observed content and search-performance signals be used to prioritize pages for human review for a possible content refresh?

Decision: Which pages should a content/SEO reviewer examine first?

The output is a prioritization aid. It is not an automatic refresh decision and does not claim that a recommended refresh will succeed.
02 · Data

A focused, public-safe slice

Release & grain

The work uses the anonymized content-refresh slice from the FlyRank ML Internship dataset. The modeling file contains 30,000 page-level observations; one row represents a content-page observation with search, content, and recent performance attributes.

Windows & exclusions

March 2026 was used as the mid-panel development window in the data-contract stage, while June 2026 was treated as a sealed outcome/test month. Client identifiers were retained only for grouped validation; outcome fields were excluded from predictive inputs.

Public-safe: No client names, private URLs, private queries, or other identifying information are disclosed here.
03 · Methodology

Simple model, honest evaluation.

Target

The modeling target is a binary proxy: trend_direction == down is treated as attention-positive; other observed directions are treated as the comparison class. This is not a label for refresh success.

Model

Logistic Regression was selected as a simple, interpretable classification method. Numeric features were imputed and standardized; competition_level was imputed and one-hot encoded.

Features

Search volume, competition level, CPC, word count, character count, 90-day impressions, clicks, pageviews, sessions, content age, and CTR.

Baseline

The Week-4 rule scores three observed signals: content age, CTR, and 90-day impressions. Pages are ranked by score and mapped to refresh, monitor, or leave actions.

Validation & leakage

The final evaluation uses an 80/20 split grouped by client_id. Training contains 23,837 rows; test contains 6,163; client overlap is 0. The model does not use trend_direction or trend_pct as features, and the feature list was checked for direct future-window or label-derived inputs.

05 · Limitations

What this work cannot claim

  1. Proxy target: downward trend is not the same thing as “needs a refresh.”
  2. Observational evidence: the study measures associations; it does not show that refreshing a page causes better search performance.
  3. Feature coverage: search intent, competitor changes, content quality, and business priorities are not fully represented.
  4. Validation scope: results come from one grouped evaluation design and may differ on other clients or periods.
  5. Moderate performance: the model improves on the baseline in the evaluated test, but neither metric is close to perfect.
06 · Ranked recommendations

Turn scores into review actions.

01

Refresh first

Prioritize pages with multiple observed refresh signals, especially pages that are relatively stale while retaining meaningful search visibility. A reviewer should verify the content and search intent before acting.

02

Monitor next

Review pages with moderate evidence, such as visibility combined with relatively weak CTR, before deciding whether a refresh is justified.

03

Leave for now

Do not prioritize pages when the available signals provide limited evidence for immediate action. Revisit when the observed signals change.

Human review is mandatory. Do not automatically publish or rewrite content, delete or redirect pages, change URLs or production SEO settings, or treat the score as proof that a refresh will improve performance.
